Bedside dashboard

A vibecoded bedside display that turns the information I check every day into one small physical interface.

Why I built it

I wanted to be able to leave my phone outside of my bedroom without missing out on the things I check anyway, like weather, subway arrivals, calendar, time, smart-light controls, and… obviously, the Mets score.

What it does

The display pulls live Mets scores, weather, subway arrivals, and my calendar, and it can switch the LIFX lights between scenes. It dims at night, protects the screen from burn-in, and falls back to the last good data when a source goes down. Bluetooth buttons and a page on my phone control it.

How I built it with AI

This is a vibecoded prototyping project, not a claim of expertise in any of these technologies. I used language models to break the idea into pieces, reason through unfamiliar libraries, debug hardware and server issues, and keep adding to it as I figured out what I wanted.

An ESP32 microcontroller drives a tiny 128×64 OLED screen, and a Python server does the rest by gathering the data, choosing what to show, and handling the buttons and phone controls.

What I learned

This is how I use AI for things I could not build alone. I did not know ESP32 firmware or BLE before this, and I still could not write either from scratch. What I could do was describe what I wanted, then test the result on real hardware and keep going until it worked.
