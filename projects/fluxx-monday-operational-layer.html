Case study

Fluxx to Monday operational layer

A controlled operational layer that surfaces grant tasks in Monday views staff will actually open, with Fluxx staying the system of record.

The problem

Fluxx holds the authoritative grants data, but it lacks project management functionality. The team needed accessible views for reports, agreements, payments, and pipeline tracking without losing data discipline.

What I built

A controlled operational layer in Monday. Records in certain states sync from Fluxx into custom Monday boards, and designated fields write back. Staff get a notification when a report or agreement changes state and can plan from views that used to be locked inside Fluxx. Nobody needs a side spreadsheet to feel sure of the status.

Object rules

ObjectSystem of recordOperational mirrorNotes
Grants / proposals Fluxx Monday, SharePoint, Power BI Fluxx is authoritative
Reports Fluxx Monday Team visibility; limited write-back of state and due date
Agreements Fluxx Monday State column only
Payments Fluxx Monday, SharePoint High-control area; Power BI depends on SharePoint list integrity
Board prep planning Monday Outlook, dashboards Operational only — not source-of-truth grants data
Meeting scheduling Monday Outlook, staging folders, board packets High manual oversight
Receipts Monday QuickBooks (manual) Intake automated; reconciliation is manual
Cash flow visuals Fluxx SharePoint → Power BI Verify upstream list health before trusting visuals

What I learned

Most of the design work went into the table above. For each object I had to decide what Monday may write back and what it may only display. Every write-back field is a place the two systems can disagree, so the list stays short and a failed sync has to be loud rather than silent.
